2 · Your §10 rulings, visualized

Q1 — Rating max: segmented control now reads 5 | 10 (see inspector above). The 7 came from research-style "opinion scales" (odd number = a neutral midpoint). It stays available on the Opinion Scale type where researchers expect 1–7; it's gone from stars, because nobody gives a hotel 7 stars.
Q2 — NPS coloring: the "Theme accent | Classic 🔴🟠🟢" toggle in the inspector. Theme accent = the chips use your theme's color (default). Classic = detractors 0–6 red, passives 7–8 amber, promoters 9–10 green — the look every NPS tool made famous.
Q3 — Emoji Scale: in v1. It's the numbered chip row wearing faces: 😠 😕 😐 🙂 😍
Q5 — Images: ruled as planned — a picture above a question stays a plain Image block you stack on top (no special per-question setting). Images inside choices (the welcome-kit tiles in the canvas) are first-class: uploaded to Salesforce Files through the existing image pipeline, snapshotted into the published spec so guest surveys never fetch at runtime.
Q6/Q7/Q8: Anonymous toggle ships in v1 (see Survey settings above) — when ON, the response record keeps scores but stores no identifying links. Image Choice allows multi-select. Signature stays available in surveys.

5 · Question Bank — what it is, what it costs L3 · your call

Plain words: a shared library of reusable questions. You write "How satisfied are you with support?" once, push it to the bank, and every future survey can insert it from the bank. Because every survey then asks the identical question, you can chart "support satisfaction across all surveys over time" — answers become comparable by construction instead of by luck.
The lift: ≈ 2–3 weeks, after v1 survey slices ship. One new object (the bank question: label, type, config, topic), a "Push to bank" button in the inspector (top-right panel), a "From Question Bank" palette source (bottom-left, shown dimmed), and a link from each answer back to its bank question — that link is what powers cross-survey charts. The one real design decision: editing a bank question freezes published usages (they keep the old wording) — silently rewording a live question would corrupt trend lines.
